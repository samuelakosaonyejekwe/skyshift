How SkyShift works

1 · Pick a place

Click the sky map, choose a featured target, or search for a name (stars, galaxies, nebulae, planets, asteroids, comets) or coordinates (83.82 -5.39, 05:35:17 -05:23:28, or galactic l=0 b=0).

2 · Watch it change

Movie plays every image in time order. Blink flips between two dates, the same trick Clyde Tombaugh used to find Pluto in 1930. Difference subtracts them so only changes light up. Colour turns infrared into a false-colour picture. Then & now compares with NASA's WISE/NEOWISE (2010–2024) and 2MASS (1997–2001) skies.

3 · Measure & hunt

The Spectrum chart shows how bright the object is at each infrared wavelength: water ice, carbon dioxide and dust leave fingerprints here. The Hunt tool looks for anything that moved or changed and checks it against 55,000+ known asteroids and comets from NASA/JPL.

What is SPHEREx?

The Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer is a NASA space telescope launched in March 2025. Every six months it maps the entire sky in 102 near-infrared colours (0.75–5 µm). Each image covers 3.5°×3.5° (about 7 full Moons across) with 6.2″ pixels.

Its special filters (linear variable filters) change colour across the detector, so as the telescope steps across the sky each star is photographed in many colours. Put together, they give a spectrum of every object, more than a billion of them.

Why "Planet X"?

Anything in our Solar System shifts position between images taken days or months apart. Nearby stars and brown dwarfs creep too. A cold, distant planet would be faint in visible light but could glow in the infrared, which is exactly what SPHEREx sees.

Tips for spotting real changes

  • Compare like with like. Choose one band (e.g. band 3) so the colour is similar between dates.
  • Asteroids move fast, usually appearing in only one image of a visit. Slow, distant objects (Kuiper Belt) shift a few pixels per day.
  • Cosmic rays and hot pixels are single sharp dots. SkyShift masks most of them using SPHEREx's quality flags.
  • Bright stars leave ghosts and halos. A real object looks like a star (same fuzzy shape) and moves in a straight line.
  • Check the Hunt list. If it says "Known", that's an asteroid or comet already catalogued by NASA/JPL.

Where the data comes from

  • SPHEREx images: NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA), read live from its public AWS bucket by your browser.
  • SPHEREx image catalogue: IRSA TAP service, re-indexed automatically every 6 hours.
  • Asteroid & comet orbits, ephemerides, close approaches: NASA/JPL Solar System Dynamics (SBDB, Horizons, CNEOS).
  • Exoplanet hosts: NASA Exoplanet Archive.
  • News & images: NASA.gov and the NASA Image and Video Library.
  • Then & now: NASA WISE/NEOWISE (unWISE) and 2MASS sky surveys via CDS HiPS. Name lookup: CDS Sesame.

Welcome to SkyShift 🔭

NASA's SPHEREx telescope photographs the entire sky over and over in infrared light. SkyShift lets you flip through those pictures to see what moves and changes.

1. Choose a spot

Click the glowing sky map or pick a featured target like the Orion Nebula or comet 3I/ATLAS, a visitor from another star.

2. Press play

Watch the images in order. Use Blink and Difference to make changes pop, and Hunt to scan for moving objects automatically.

3. Take it anywhere
